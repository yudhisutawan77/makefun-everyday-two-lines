## Entry

> A traffic jam is a ketchup bottle tipped upside down: everyone squeezes, nothing comes out, then suddenly it all rushes at once.
> It maps the long wait and the sudden rush, but leaves out that no hand is squeezing the cars, and ketchup never gets angry.

253 characters (including the trailing newline), two lines, written to `/output/entry.txt` by `main.py`.

## Why this analogy

A 10-year-old has held a ketchup bottle and knows exactly what happens when
it is tipped upside down: nothing, nothing, then everything at once. That is
precisely how a traffic jam feels from inside a car — long stillness, then a
sudden burst of movement — and no other everyday object in the kitchen
captures that stop-start rhythm so exactly. The bottle is concrete (you can
see it, hold it, squeeze it) and the pairing is surprising: nobody expects
traffic to be explained with condiments.

Line 2 stays honest instead of praising the analogy: it names what is mapped
(the long wait and the sudden rush) and what is left out (there is no hand
squeezing the cars, and ketchup never gets angry — drivers do). That keeps
the picture from giving a child the wrong idea about why jams happen.

## How it runs

`main.py` (Python 3, stdlib only, deterministic, no network) validates the
entry against the sealed gate (1–400 UTF-8 characters, not whitespace-only)
and writes it to `ENTRY_OUT_DIR/entry.txt`, or `ENTRY_OUT`, or
`/output/entry.txt` by default.
